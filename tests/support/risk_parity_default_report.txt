A replication against data is exploratory by construction. The sample was spent
on a hypothesis Qian chose and Chan reported, so this run says whether his
figures reproduce and nothing about whether either allocation is a good idea.
The 4% is Chan's constant applied to whatever window was read, not a rate
anyone paid, and no transaction cost or financing spread is charged, which
favours the levered portfolio the book argues for.

The assumed rate is not neutral to the ranking, which is why every window above
reports what it costs. The Sharpe difference changes with the rate by
1/vol(60/40) less 1/vol(risk parity, unlevered), and levering to match collapses
that to (1 - leverage) / volatility. So a higher assumed rate penalises risk
parity whenever the leverage is above 1. --risk-free moves the rate, and a run
that does is off the reproduction, because the rate was declared with the
windows.
docs/replication-log.md Entry 4 carries the verdict.
